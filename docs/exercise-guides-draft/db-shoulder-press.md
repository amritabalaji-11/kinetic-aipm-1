## Top of page
- **One-line summary:** Press two dumbbells straight up from your shoulders, using your shoulders and arms, not your legs. The overhead press to learn first.
- **Level:** Beginner – Intermediate.
- **Equipment:** Two dumbbells. **[CONFIRM]** A bench with back support makes it easier (see "Make it easier").
- **Main muscles:** Shoulders, triceps.
- **Helping muscles:** Upper chest, upper back, core.
- **Starting weight:** Start light, a weight you could press 12 times with good form. Add weight only when every rep stays controlled and you don't arch your back.

## Quick view (the "Cues" card, 15 seconds)
1. Sit or stand. Standing: press the floor away, hips and knees stay still. The power comes from your shoulders. (Sitting takes your legs out of it.)
2. Dumbbells side by side, palms forward, forearms straight up. Elbows a little in front of you, not out wide. (If palms forward pinches your shoulder, turn them in a little.)
3. Press straight up until the dumbbells are over your shoulders. Don't arch your back.

Cues approved by Amrit, 12 Nov 2026. **[CONFIRM]** with a trainer: cues 2 and 3 (dumbbell angle, palm direction, straight path). No source compared parallel and angled dumbbells or described the dumbbell path.

- **Breathing:** Breathe in and brace before each press, out as you press up. **[CONFIRM]** Sources differ on exact breathing; this matches the goblet squat wording.
- **Tempo:** 1 second up, 1 second at the top, 2 seconds down.
- **Sets and reps:** 3 sets of 8–12.

## How to do it
**Setup**
1. Pick up the dumbbells and bring them to shoulder height, side by side, palms facing forward (or turned in a little if that pinches), elbows a little in front of your body.
2. Stand with feet about hip-width apart, whole foot planted, knees soft but still. (Or sit on a bench with a back, feet flat.)
3. Squeeze your glutes, brace your core and pull your ribs down so your lower back stays flat.
4. Keep your wrists straight over your elbows, forearms upright.

**Movement**
1. Breathe in, then press both dumbbells straight up until they are over your shoulders and your arms are almost straight. Don't lock out hard.
2. Keep your head still and your ribs down. If your lower back starts to arch, stop a little lower.
3. Lower slowly, about 2 seconds, back to shoulder height. Elbows stay under your wrists.
4. Legs stay still the whole time. Don't dip your knees or bounce to get the weight up.

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Lower back arches, ribs flare | Squeeze your glutes and pull your ribs down. Use a lighter weight, or sit with back support. |
| Legs help push the weight up (knees dip and straighten) | Keep your knees soft and still. If the weight only moves with leg help, it is too heavy. |
| Elbows flare wide | Keep your elbows under your wrists, a little in front of your body. |
| Wrists bend back | Stack your wrist over your elbow, with the weight resting on your palm base. |
| Pushing your head forward as the weight passes | Keep your chin tucked slightly and your neck long. |
| Dumbbells crash together at the top | Press straight up and keep them apart. Stop short of a hard lockout. |
| Dropping the weight quickly | Take 2 seconds to lower it. |

## Make it easier
| Step | What changes | Pick this if… |
|---|---|---|
| **Seated Shoulder Press** (new) | Sit on a bench with back support, feet flat. | You arch your back, or lose balance standing. |
| **Lighter weight, same press** | Drop weight until every rep is smooth. | The last reps need leg help or a back arch. |

## Make it harder
| Step | What changes | Pick this if… |
|---|---|---|
| **Tempo Press** (new) | 3 seconds down, 1 second pause at shoulders. | You can do 12 clean reps. |
| **Arnold Press** (`ex_039`) | Rotate the palms as you press. | You can do 12 clean reps and want more shoulder work. |
| **DB Push Press** (`ex_043`) | A small leg dip and drive helps the press. | **Different exercise.** Use it when you want to lift heavier with leg help, on purpose. |

## Safety
- Stop if you feel sharp pain or pinching at the top of your shoulder.
- Take care with a shoulder, neck or lower back injury. Ask a doctor or physio before adding weight.
- With high blood pressure or a heart condition, ask your doctor before bracing hard and holding your breath.
- Control the weights and don't drop them from height. A spotter helps with heavy sets.

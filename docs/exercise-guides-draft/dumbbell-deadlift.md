# DB Deadlift — guide content (DRAFT, not reviewed by a trainer)

**[CONFIRM] Which version?** Your plans list "KB Deadlift ... or DB Deadlifts", which suggests one weight held between the feet. This draft covers the more common version: **two dumbbells held in front of your thighs**. The single vertical dumbbell or kettlebell version (`ex_095` KB Deadlift) would be an "easier" step below.

## Top of page
- **One-line summary:** Push your hips back, lower the weights down your legs with a flat back, then stand tall by driving your hips forward.
- **Level:** Beginner to intermediate. Learn the hip hinge with no weight first.
- **Equipment:** Two dumbbells.
- **Main muscles:** Glutes, hamstrings.
- **Helping muscles:** Lower back (holds your position), upper back, core, forearms (grip).
- **Starting weight:** Light. Use a weight you can control for 8–12 reps (ACE). Add weight slowly once the hinge feels automatic.

## Quick view (the "Next up" card, 15 seconds)
1. Feet hip-width, knees soft. Big breath in, brace, back long.
2. Push your hips back. The weights slide down your legs. Keep your back flat.
3. Push the floor away, squeeze your glutes at the top. Don't lean back.

- **Breathing:** Breathe in and brace before you lower, stay tight on the way down, breathe out as you stand. **[CONFIRM]** Sources differ: some say breathe out while lowering and in while lifting; others brace and hold. ACE's deadlift article gives no breathing instruction.
- **Tempo:** 2 seconds down, 1 second reset at the bottom, then up in about 1 second.
- **Sets and reps:** 2–3 sets of 8–12.

## How to do it
**Setup**
1. Stand with feet about hip-width apart, a slight bend in your knees.
2. Hold a dumbbell in each hand in front of your thighs, palms facing your body.
3. Pull your shoulders back and down. Chest lifted, neck in line with your spine.
4. Take a big breath in and brace your core.

**Movement**
1. Push your hips straight back (like closing a door behind you with your bottom). Let the dumbbells slide down the front of your legs, close to your body.
2. Keep your back long and flat. Your knees bend a little, but this is not a squat.
3. Lower until the dumbbells reach about mid-shin, or as far as you can keep your back flat. Stop there.
4. Push the floor away through your feet and drive your hips forward to stand. Your shoulders and hips rise together.
5. At the top, squeeze your glutes and stand tall. Don't lean back.

## Common mistakes
| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Rounding the back | Lower less far. Practise the hinge with no weight first. |
| Turning it into a squat (knees bend a lot, hips drop low) | Push your hips back first; your shins stay almost vertical. |
| Dumbbells drift away from your legs | Keep them brushing your legs the whole way. |
| Pulling with the arms or shoulders | Arms stay long and loose. The strength comes from your legs and hips. |
| Leaning back at the top | Stand tall and squeeze your glutes. Stop when your hips are fully forward. |
| Looking up or craning the neck | Keep your neck in line with your spine. Look at the floor a few steps ahead. |
| Rushing the setup, or too heavy too soon | Take a moment to brace. Use a weight you control for every rep. |

## Make it easier
| Step | What changes | Pick this if… |
|---|---|---|
| **Wall Hip Hinge** (new) | No weight. Stand a foot from a wall with your back to it, push your hips back until your bottom taps the wall, then stand. | You can't keep your back flat, or feel it in your lower back. Do 10–15 good hinges before adding weights (ACE). |
| **Raised Deadlift** (new) | Rest the dumbbells on blocks or sturdy books so you start higher. | You can hinge with no weight but can't reach mid-shin with a flat back. |
| **KB Deadlift** (`ex_095`) | One kettlebell or vertical dumbbell, held between your feet. | Two dumbbells feel awkward or you want a lighter start. |

## Make it harder
| Step | What changes | Pick this if… |
|---|---|---|
| **DB Romanian Deadlift** (`ex_048`) | Knees stay soft and don't bend more, lower to mid-shin, and don't set the weights down between reps. | You can do 12 clean deadlifts and want more hamstring work. |
| **One-Arm DB Deadlift** (new) | One dumbbell at your side, same hinge. The uneven load works your hips and core harder. | You can do 12 clean reps with two dumbbells. Do the same reps each side. |

## Safety
- Stop if you feel sharp pain in your back, or pain running down a leg.
- If you've had back pain or an injury, ask a doctor or physio before loading this.
- ACE's advice: "If the weight doesn't feel right, leave it on the floor." Control the weights; don't drop them from height.
- If you have high blood pressure or a heart condition, ask your doctor before bracing hard and holding your breath.
